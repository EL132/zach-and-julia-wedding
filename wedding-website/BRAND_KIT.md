# Brand Kit: Julia & Zach Wedding Website

## Brand Direction

The visual direction for the wedding website should be:

**European Garden Estate Editorial**

The site should feel elegant, romantic, botanical, refined, and personal. The overall mood should be inspired by European villas, château gardens, formal outdoor dinners, soft white florals, manicured greenery, antique stationery, and cinematic black-and-white photography.

The website should not feel like a generic modern wedding template. It should feel like a quiet luxury estate wedding: old-world, garden-focused, polished, intimate, and editorial.

The home page should feel especially cinematic and photo-led. It should act as an emotional entry point into the wedding weekend: a black-and-white editorial story of Zach and Julia, with spacious typography, scattered photography, and a restrained transition into the Maryhill Estate setting.

## Core Vibe

Use these words as the guiding brand language:

* European
* Garden
* Estate
* Editorial
* Cinematic
* Romantic
* Refined
* Botanical
* Formal
* Warm
* Timeless
* Personal
* Old-world, but fresh

The site should feel formal enough for a wedding, but not cold or corporate. It should be romantic, but not overly cute. It should be beautiful and polished, but still personal and approachable.

## What the Brand Should Feel Like

The website should feel like:

> A refined European garden estate wedding website with cinematic black-and-white photography, warm ivory paper tones, layered sage and moss greens, classical serif typography, understated botanical details, and spacious editorial layouts.

The overall impression should be soft, elegant, timeless, and quietly luxurious.

The home page should feel like a black-and-white editorial wedding announcement: cinematic, spacious, emotional, and restrained. The interior pages can lean more into the warm ivory, green, and botanical estate palette.

## Home Page Direction

The home page should be treated as a distinct visual moment within the broader brand.

It should be:

* Primarily black and white
* Photo-led
* Cinematic
* Editorial
* Spacious
* Minimal in copy
* Romantic without being overly decorative
* Refined without feeling stiff

The home page should open with a full-width cover photo that fills the landing view. Below that, the page should continue as an editorial scroll story with scattered black-and-white photos, quiet serif typography, generous spacing, and restrained calls to action.

The home page should not feel like a standard marketing landing page with many content blocks. It should feel more like a magazine spread or a refined wedding photo essay.

### Home Page Layout Principles

The recommended home page structure is:

1. **Full-width cinematic hero**

   * Use the cover photo as the first visual moment.
   * Overlay only minimal text, such as “Zach & Julia” and a small date/location line.
   * Keep the overlay elegant and readable.

2. **Quiet editorial intro**

   * Use a black or near-black background.
   * Include one short centered serif line.
   * Example: “A weekend in the gardens of Maryhill Estate.”
   * Add a small uppercase supporting label beneath it.

3. **Editorial photo story**

   * Scatter the remaining black-and-white photos throughout the page.
   * Avoid a uniform grid.
   * Alternate image sizes and alignments.
   * Use generous negative space.
   * Keep photo presentation clean, rectangular, and refined.

4. **Maryhill Estate transition**

   * Use the Maryhill Estate drawing lower on the page as a tasteful transition into the broader wedding setting.
   * This can be the first warm/color moment on the home page.
   * Present it like an elegant printed estate insert.

5. **Final call to action**

   * Close with a simple centered section.
   * Suggested copy: “Join us at Maryhill Estate.”
   * Include restrained links or buttons for RSVP, Weekend Details, and Travel.

## Interior Page Direction

Interior pages should continue the same refined estate feeling, but can use more of the full brand palette.

Interior pages may use:

* Warm ivory backgrounds
* Soft cream cards and sections
* Sage, olive, and moss green accents
* Thin dividers and borders
* Botanical details
* Serif headings
* Clean sans-serif body text

The interior pages should feel connected to the home page, but less cinematic and more informational. They should be elegant, readable, and easy to navigate.

## What to Avoid

Avoid design choices that feel:

* Too trendy
* Too playful
* Too techy
* Too bright
* Too pink
* Too modern-minimalist
* Too luxury-hotel / Gatsby
* Too rustic
* Too boho
* Too scrapbook-like
* Too generic wedding-template

Avoid relying heavily on:

* Script fonts
* Bright white backgrounds
* Harsh black text on warm pages
* Neon or overly saturated greens
* Heavy shadows
* Thick borders
* Decorative wedding icons
* Fake polaroids, tape, stickers, or tilted scrapbook effects
* Overly animated or gimmicky interactions

When in doubt, choose restraint.

The home page is an exception: it should be primarily black and white, with soft off-white text and only minimal warm/color moments.

## Color Distribution

### Home Page

The home page should be restrained and cinematic.

Recommended distribution:

* **80% black / near-black / black-and-white photography**
* **15% warm white / soft white typography**
* **5% warm estate color moment**, primarily through the Maryhill Estate drawing or a very restrained accent

Do not introduce heavy greens, blush, or gold on the home page. Let the black-and-white photography carry the emotion.

### Interior Pages

Interior pages should follow the broader estate palette.

Recommended distribution:

* **70% ivory / cream**
* **20% greens**
* **8% dark text**
* **2% blush / antique gold accents**

The website should be primarily ivory, cream, sage, olive, moss, and espresso outside of the home page.

Blush and gold should be used sparingly. They should act as quiet supporting accents, not primary brand colors. Too much blush will make the site feel like a generic wedding website. Too much gold will make it feel overly glamorous or Gatsby-inspired.

## Color Usage Guidance

### Home Page Backgrounds

Use black or near-black backgrounds on the home page.

Avoid pure white sections on the home page unless there is a very intentional transition. The black-and-white editorial mood should remain consistent until the Maryhill Estate drawing introduces the broader estate palette.

### Interior Page Backgrounds

Use warm ivory as the main background color. Use soft cream for sections, cards, and areas that need gentle contrast.

The site should avoid pure white as the dominant background. The warmer ivory and cream tones are important for the antique paper, garden estate feeling.

### Text

On the home page, use warm white or soft off-white text instead of harsh pure white.

On interior pages, use deep moss or espresso for text rather than pure black.

Headings can use deep moss. Body text should generally use espresso for readability.

### Buttons and Links

Home page buttons should be minimal, refined, and restrained. Use transparent or dark backgrounds with thin warm-white borders, or soft warm-white buttons with dark text.

Interior page primary buttons should use deep moss or olive green with ivory text.

Secondary buttons can use transparent or cream backgrounds with moss borders and moss text.

### Accents

Use sage for soft borders, dividers, hover states, small decorative elements, and subtle section details.

Use blush and antique gold only for small details, such as a thin rule, monogram accent, tiny icon, or hover detail.

## Typography Direction

The typography should be serif-forward, elegant, and editorial.

Recommended type direction:

* **Display / Headings:** Cormorant Garamond
* **Body / UI:** Inter
* **Optional Accent:** Parisienne or no script font at all

The brand should not depend on script typography. The elegance should primarily come from serif typography, spacing, color, and imagery.

## Typography Personality

The heading font should feel classical, romantic, and old-world. It should carry the wedding elegance.

The body font should be clean, readable, and modern enough to keep the website usable. It should not compete with the display font.

The optional accent or script font should be used extremely sparingly, if used at all.

## Typography Usage Guidance

### Hero Names

The couple’s names should be one of the strongest visual moments on the home page.

Use large, elegant serif typography. The names should feel editorial, romantic, and cinematic, not overly decorative.

Possible treatments:

* `Zach & Julia`
* `ZACH & JULIA`
* `Zach and Julia`

Avoid overly ornate script treatments.

### Headings

Use large, elegant serif headings with generous spacing.

Headings should feel editorial and refined, not overly bold or decorative.

### Body Text

Body text should be simple, readable, and calm.

Use a clean sans-serif font for paragraphs, labels, navigation, RSVP forms, travel information, and schedule details.

### Labels and Navigation

Use small uppercase sans-serif text with subtle letter spacing for navigation, section labels, metadata, and buttons.

This will help the site feel formal and polished without feeling cluttered.

## Photography Direction

Photography is central to the visual identity.

The home page should use the black-and-white photos as the main design material. The photos should feel cinematic, documentary, romantic, and personal.

Photo presentation should be:

* Spacious
* Editorial
* Clean
* Asymmetrical
* Lightly varied in scale
* Balanced with negative space

Avoid:

* Uniform photo grids
* Heavy image borders
* Thick frames
* Drop shadows
* Polaroid effects
* Tilted scrapbook layouts
* Overlapping images unless extremely subtle
* Busy captions

The images should feel placed with intention, like a magazine editorial.

## Motion and Interaction

Motion should be subtle and restrained.

Acceptable motion:

* Gentle fade-ins
* Slight upward reveal on scroll
* Soft hover states on links/buttons
* Slow, elegant transitions

Avoid:

* Bouncy animations
* Excessive parallax
* Heavy scroll effects
* Playful hover gimmicks
* Interactions that make the site feel techy or distracting

The website should feel calm and timeless.

## Layout Principles

The site should prioritize:

* Spacious layouts
* Large serif typography
* Strong photography
* Editorial composition
* Warm neutral backgrounds on interior pages
* Soft green accents
* Thin dividers and borders
* Understated botanical details
* Elegant simplicity
* Clear navigation
* Mobile readability

The site should feel carefully designed, but not overdesigned.

## Component Style

### Cards

Cards should be simple, warm, and refined.

Use soft cream or warm ivory backgrounds with thin borders. Avoid heavy shadows. Rounded corners should be subtle, if used at all.

### Dividers

Use thin lines, small botanical marks, or generous whitespace instead of heavy section separators.

### Buttons

Buttons should feel formal and quiet.

Use small uppercase text with letter spacing. Avoid large, loud, modern SaaS-style buttons.

### Forms

Forms should feel clean and elegant.

Use warm backgrounds, thin borders, clear labels, and readable spacing. Avoid overly modern input styling that feels disconnected from the estate brand.

## Copy Tone

Copy should be warm, concise, and refined.

It should feel personal but not overly casual.

Use short, elegant phrases where possible.

Examples:

* “A weekend in the gardens of Maryhill Estate.”
* “Join us at Maryhill Estate.”
* “Celebrate with us in Glumslöv.”
* “Weekend Details”
* “Travel”
* “RSVP”

Avoid copy that feels overly cute, gimmicky, or generic.

## Final Design Principle

The site should feel like a cinematic black-and-white wedding editorial that opens into a refined European garden estate experience.

The home page should create emotion and atmosphere.

The interior pages should provide clarity and detail.

Across the whole site, the guiding principle is:

**Restraint, romance, and editorial elegance.**
